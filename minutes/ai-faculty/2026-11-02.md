## Community college recruiting

- The Chattanooga State visit went well. Several students were interested, and the presenter was invited back to any class. Showing how their courses map to ours worked well.
- A Pellissippi visit is in three weeks. Two other schools will be followed up next semester.
- Idea: a five-year pathway of 2 years at community college, then the bachelor's and master's. A draft exists but isn't ready to present yet. The group thinks it would be very popular.
- The recruiting presentation was moved to the next meeting.

## AI majors: numbers and community

- The current count is 32 AI majors, up from 26 (about 20%).
- The faculty want to get to know the majors better, through events that build community and by tracking where graduates go.
- Suggested: look at how AI majors are doing academically, and consider more proactive advising.
- **Spring:** a camping trip, around April. Two faculty must attend, and the department writes its own waiver. Campus can lend tents and gear.
- **This semester:** an event for AI majors after Thanksgiving (dead week). Food alone is a weak draw at that time of year.
  - J. Roberts noted that a chance to meet employers or industry contacts would draw students most, since many are career-focused. Ideas: ask the advisory board who'll be in the area, or invite a speaker from a large in-state employer.
  - Settled on a **grill-out**, possibly at the pavilion by the beach volleyball area near the softball and baseball fields. It needs a reservation.
- **Funding:** the group will confirm what MInDS (Machine Intelligence and Data Science center) funds are available for student-oriented events.

## AI minor and accessibility

- The current AI minor's prerequisites make it workable only for technical majors (CS, cyber, ECE, mechatronics, math). A more accessible *applied* AI minor would need different courses, not the upper-division AI courses.
- The AI minor proposal is going to the undergraduate curriculum committee. It was emailed out, and it'll be forwarded to anyone who didn't get it.

## University Curriculum Committee

- Everything passed.
- The committee asked why storytelling is 2 hours. The answer: that's what fit within the 15 elective hours.
- A question about 4510 having 4310 as a prerequisite: the second digit marks the area (AI, ML, data science), not the level.

## Replacing 2310

- Capstone faculty report that students still struggle with programming, and that 2310 is no longer the heavy coding course it once was.
- The group revisited replacing 2310 in the AI curriculum with an AI-focused programming course ("AI development and design"). The proposal is worded to explain what AI majors need, not to criticize 2310.
- Suggestion: add systems integration (how the parts of AI software fit together), possibly as a project. There was general support.
- The new course will be **3 hours**. Storytelling remains **2 hours** for now.
- 2310 isn't on the community college pathway, so transfer students aren't affected.
- Next: integration is added, the group reviews it, and it goes to the undergraduate curriculum committee as a memo.

## Teaching plan

- D. Talbert showed the teaching-plan spreadsheet. Adding the new course meant swapping out a graduate section to stay at 10 sections (5 faculty, 2 each a semester). Cutting AI 2000 to one fall section is another option.
- About 4 PhD-level slots a year remain after the 6210/6220 sequence.
- On PhD students teaching: one view was that they need teaching experience for jobs at R2 schools. The concern was that AI 2000 is the gateway course where students decide whether AI is for them, so 2300 or the new course might suit better.
- Outcome: add the course if it's right, and make the staffing work, including with PhD instructors if needed.

## Certificates

- Agreed: create a **data science certificate**, and probably an **ML certificate**, with the same structure. A student completes the courses plus an opt-in, pass/fail culminating project added to a course such as data engineering or storytelling.
- This recognizes the students who choose it, keeps elective hours free for minors, and complements the broad "AI" degree name.
- A data science minor isn't planned at this time.

## Capstone and ABET

- There are differing readings of whether ABET requires a two-semester capstone or a capstone that builds on earlier semesters. The group may ask for clarification.
